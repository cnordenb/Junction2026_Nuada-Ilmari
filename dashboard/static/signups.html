Free-tier guard

Free plans attract bot networks that open hundreds of accounts for phishing, spam and abuse. This tool spots those swarms of fake signups. It runs per-signup checks, then groups accounts that share rare details and tests whether their timing beats chance. Every verdict comes with its reasons, and we publish our own error rates.

1. Try it live: sign up for a pretend free plan

Your signup joins the end of a simulated two-week signup stream (3,500 accounts). Try one normal signup, then try to farm accounts: five or more quick signups, or addresses like user48213@mailinator.com, or j.o.h.n.doe+1@gmail.com followed by johndoe+2@gmail.com. Your test signups are kept in memory for this browser only and deleted after two hours.

2. Analyse a signup stream

The demo stream is simulated: 3,000 ordinary signups, five planted abuse campaigns, and innocent look-alikes (a class signing up together, a company team, a newsletter rush, families on one laptop, VPN users). An answer key lets you see exactly what the detector got right and wrong.

3. How accurate is it?

Measured on five fresh simulated streams (not the one we designed against), and on five "hard" streams where attackers use tricks we did not tune for: throwaway domains missing from our list, normal browsers, 20 devices instead of 4, looser timing. These are simulated data written by our own team, so they show the logic works as designed and where it breaks. They are not a measurement on a real platform.

4. How it decides

Per-signup checks (points)

  • Throwaway inbox service: 45
  • Several accounts on one inbox (Gmail ignores dots and +tags): 50
  • Browser says it is an automation tool: 50
  • One device behind 5+ accounts: 35
  • Datacenter or VPN network: 10 (weak on purpose: privacy users)
  • Device check did not run: 10

60+ points: block. 35+: review.

Swarm layer

  • Two signups are linked when they share enough rare details: device, real inbox, email pattern, username pattern, exact browser build, IP, address block, network. Details shared by more than 3% of all signups (gmail.com, the current Chrome) are ignored.
  • Each group's timing is compared with 4,000 random groups from the same stream: a tight burst, a timer, or the same time every night.
  • Block only with shared identity details plus unusual timing, or a network/browser match plus strongly unusual timing, or high per-signup risk. A member is blocked only if it carries the evidence itself.
  • A shared school or company email domain with no shared devices means an organisation. It goes to review and is never blocked.
